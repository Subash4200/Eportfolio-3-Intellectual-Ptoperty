Artefact 1 – Copyright and Artificial Intelligence Reference Group (CAIRG), 2024

Summary

The Copyright and Artificial Intelligence Reference Group (CAIRG) is an Australian Government initiative to address new copyright challenges arising from the use of artificial intelligence. It discusses these issues related to the use of copyright content as feed to AI systems and transparency on AI. The development of AI is raising new issues for copyright law and policy. It applies to the Week 7 workshop as the workshop discusses that the expression of original works is protected while AI has brought uncertainty to protection and ownership of AI-related works. The CAIRG is thus an up-to-date Australian illustration of the way in which intellectual property policy is adapting to technological change (Attorney-General's Department, 2024).

Justification

I have chosen this artefact for being relevant to a recent Australian policy response to copyright and AI, and to the workshop discussion. It made me realize that intellectual property is not just a protection for creators but also a balancing of the rights of creators and innovation and public good. AI systems can interact with copyrighted content in their development or use, especially for ICT professionals. The artefact shows an awareness of legal and ethical responsibilities in the context of responsible technology practice. It is meaningful evidence as it demonstrates how a contemporary ICT issue can have a wider impact on creators, organisations and society.

Artefact 3 – IP Australia, Artificial Intelligence – Inventorship and Entitlement, 2025

Summary

This guidance from IP Australia outlines how the Australian laws apply in the case of an invention that uses AI. As per the guidance, an inventor under Australian patent law must be a natural person and AI cannot be named as the inventor. It talks about the case of Thaler, where an artificial intelligence system called DABUS was made the inventor, and states that the Australian courts have ruled that an inventor needs to be a person. The guidance clarified in January 2025 and thus meets the assessment criterion for a modern artefact (IP Australia, 2025).

Justification

I chose this artefact because it helps me to broaden my understanding of intellectual property, beyond copyright to patents. Patents grant exclusive rights to inventions and AI presents challenging questions around authorship and ownership, the Week 7 workshop will explain. Some of this artefact has led me to the appreciation that AI can serve as a tool for innovation, but its inventorship is still associated with human contributors. This is significant to my future ICT career as technological development can make inventions that organisations want to protect. The artefact is used to show that the world of innovation has a legal dimension touching upon the ICT professional beyond just the technical aspect.
